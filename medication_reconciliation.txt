Medication Reconciliation on Hospital Discharge — Example Practice Protocol

Purpose: to reduce medication errors when a patient returns to practice care after a hospital admission.

Process:
1. The practice assistant flags any discharge letter received for a same-day medication review, not the next routine appointment.
2. The reviewing doctor compares the discharge medication list against the practice's current record within 48 hours of receipt.
3. Any discrepancy (drug stopped, started, or dose changed) is discussed with the patient by phone or at a booked appointment before the next prescription is issued.
4. Discrepancies involving high-risk medications (anticoagulants, insulin, opioids) are reviewed same-day, not within 48 hours.
5. The updated medication list is confirmed with the patient and documented in the record.

Escalation contact: reviewing doctor; high-risk discrepancies escalate to the practice's designated medicines-safety lead.

Review: this example protocol would be reviewed every six months in a real practice, aligned with local pharmacy audit cycles.
